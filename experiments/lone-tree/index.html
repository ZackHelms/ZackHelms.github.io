LONE TREE — HOW IT WORKS

WHAT YOU'RE LOOKING AT

A single procedurally grown tree on a grassy rise, under a late-morning sun with drifting clouds. The picker at the top switches between six species — English oak, silver birch, weeping willow, Italian cypress, Japanese maple and Scots pine — each rebuilt from its own parameter preset: skeleton proportions, branch angles, leaf shape and colour, bark recipe. Zoom is unrestricted — pinch or scroll right up to the trunk axis and the bark holds up. The breeze is always working: the trunk leans a little, branches sway on their own rhythms, twigs flutter, and every leaf trembles individually. When a leaf's face swings through the mirror angle between the sun and your eye it fires a brief specular glint — watch the canopy sparkle when a gust passes. When a cloud slides across the sun the whole scene's direct light dims and the tree's shadow fades, leaving ambient sky light. Drag to walk around the tree, pinch or scroll to zoom.

SIX SPECIES, ONE GENERATOR

The species presets follow the tree knowledge base in the zmhstudio repo (templates/design/trees/), researched and maintained by a dedicated tree-expert agent: per-species articles on silhouette, branching architecture, bark and foliage, each ending in a generator-parameters table this page's presets mirror. The generator reads per-order branch counts, insertion angles, length ratios, droop, and habit flags — branches along the trunk for the columnar cypress and the bare-trunked pine, hanging curtain branches for the willow, a low limb for the oak. Leaf shapes are separate texture recipes: lobed oval (oak), pointed oval (birch), lanceolate (willow), five-lobed palmate (maple), a stroke-drawn scale spray (cypress) and a needle tuft card (pine).

GROWING THE TREE

  • A recursive branching walk builds the skeleton at load: trunk → 5 limbs → branches → twigs, ~200 branches in all. Each branch is three slightly-bent tapered tube segments (6–8 sides near the trunk, 3 at the twigs), all appended into one static mesh.
  • Each vertex carries wind metadata: a flex weight (how far out on the tree it is — trunk barely moves, twigs move a lot), a per-branch phase so branches don't sway in lockstep, and its normalized height for whole-tree lean.
  • Every leaf is anchored by its stem to a point on a branch: leaves are laid out along the outer branches in a golden-angle (phyllotaxis) spiral plus a small fan at each twig tip — several thousand in all, each with a random length, outward direction, tint, gloss and curl. They render as one instanced draw call of a six-vertex strip, so each blade can fold along its midrib and curl at the tip instead of being a flat card.
  • Nothing floats: a leaf's geometry grows from its anchor point outward, its flutter is a small rotation hinged at that anchor, and the anchor rides the same wind displacement as the twig it sits on — so the canopy moves as attached foliage, not a particle cloud.

SURFACE DETAIL: NORMAL MAPS FROM HEIGHT FIELDS

Both materials follow the zmh-3d scenery pipeline's "texture recipe" idea — procedural height fields turned into albedo + normal-map pairs at load (Sobel gradient → tangent-space normals). The bark recipe builds wandering vertical ridges, crack bands and lenticel speckle, seamless around the circumference; every branch tube carries UVs (girth-scaled so ridge density stays even from trunk to twig) and a ring tangent, and the fragment shader lights the mapped normal, so ridges catch the sun on one side and shade on the other. The leaf recipe encodes a domed blade cross-section, a midrib groove and side-vein ripples; combined with the geometric fold and curl, a "flat" quad shades like a curved living leaf, and glints sweep across it per-pixel rather than flashing the whole card.

WIND, PER VERTEX (DELIBERATELY SUBTLE)

Nothing about the sway is animated on the CPU — the meshes never change. A vertex shader displaces everything per frame: a very slow whole-tree lean (weighted by height²), a small per-branch sway (sin(t·f + phase) × flex), and for leaves a gentle rock about their own stem, hinged at the anchor. A JavaScript gust model (slow sine + a smoothed random walk) feeds the shaders one wind-strength number. The amplitudes are tuned low on purpose: the scene should read as a calm day where the appearance carries the realism and the breeze is just alive enough to notice.

LIGHT: SUN, CLOUDS, GLINTS

  • Sky: a fullscreen shader raycasts each pixel: gradient sky, sun disc + glow, and two octaves of drifting value-noise clouds sampled on a high plane, with silver-lining brightening near the sun.
  • The cloud trick: the same 256² noise data lives twice — as the GPU texture the sky shader draws, and as a JavaScript array. Each frame the CPU samples the cloud density on the sun→tree line and passes a sunVisibility uniform; every material multiplies its direct light by it. So when you see a cloud reach the sun, the ground really darkens, leaf glints die down, and the shadow softens — all from one shared noise field.
  • Leaf shading: per-leaf diffuse from its fluttering normal, a translucency term (leaves between you and the sun glow lime from behind), and a Blinn–Phong glint (pow(N·H, ~90)) with per-leaf gloss — the flutter constantly sweeps normals through the highlight, which is exactly why real trees sparkle.
  • Ground: noise-blotched grass, sun + sky ambient, a soft elliptical blob shadow under the canopy (strength follows sun visibility), and distance haze that fades the hill into the horizon colour.

APPROXIMATIONS & LIMITATIONS

  • No real shadowing: the canopy shadow is a painted ellipse, leaves don't shade each other or the branches, and there's no dappled light on the ground.
  • The wind is kinematic — sines and noise, not fluid dynamics or spring physics, so branches never load up and release the way heavy limbs do in strong gusts.
  • Leaves are six-vertex strips: they fold and curl, but still bend only at the stem when they flutter — no mid-blade flexing — and their alpha-tested edges are hard against the sky (no soft translucent silhouettes).
  • The normal maps change shading only — leaf and bark silhouettes stay polygonal, and bark UV repeats can mismatch by one ridge where tube segments of different girth meet.
  • Clouds are a flat 2D field on one altitude plane — no volume, no cloud bottoms catching light differently at the horizon.
  • Branch normals aren't re-bent by the sway displacement, so bark lighting is slightly stale mid-gust (invisible in practice at these amplitudes).
  • One tree, one LOD: a few hundred branch tubes + up to ~14,000 leaf instances every frame regardless of distance.
  • Species are stylised toward their recognisable adult forms; a real specimen varies far more (age, wind exposure, soil), and the pine's two-tone trunk is one texture, not a height-dependent material.

TOOLING

Hand-written single-file HTML/JS/GLSL — four draw calls (sky, ground, branches, leaves), no libraries, no assets; the tree, leaf sprite, noise field and hill are all generated in the page at load.
