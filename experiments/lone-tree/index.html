LONE TREE — HOW IT WORKS

WHAT YOU'RE LOOKING AT

A single procedurally grown tree on a grassy rise, under a late-morning sun with drifting clouds. The breeze is always working: the trunk leans a little, branches sway on their own rhythms, twigs flutter, and every leaf trembles individually. When a leaf's face swings through the mirror angle between the sun and your eye it fires a brief specular glint — watch the canopy sparkle when a gust passes. When a cloud slides across the sun the whole scene's direct light dims and the tree's shadow fades, leaving ambient sky light. Drag to walk around the tree, pinch or scroll to zoom.

GROWING THE TREE

  • A recursive branching walk builds the skeleton at load: trunk → 5 limbs → branches → twigs, ~200 branches in all. Each branch is three slightly-bent tapered tube segments (6–8 sides near the trunk, 3 at the twigs), all appended into one static mesh.
  • Each vertex carries wind metadata: a flex weight (how far out on the tree it is — trunk barely moves, twigs move a lot), a per-branch phase so branches don't sway in lockstep, and its normalized height for whole-tree lean.
  • Leaves attach where twigs end (plus along outer branches): ~3,000 of them, each a small textured quad with a random size, orientation, colour tint and gloss. They render as one instanced draw call; the leaf shape is an alpha-tested canvas texture (pointed oval with a centre vein), so no per-frame sorting is needed.

WIND, PER VERTEX

Nothing about the sway is animated on the CPU — the meshes never change. A vertex shader displaces everything per frame: a slow whole-tree lean (weighted by height²), a per-branch sway (sin(t·f + phase) × flex), and for leaves an extra high-frequency flutter that rotates each leaf about its own axis. A JavaScript gust model (slow sine + a smoothed random walk) feeds the shaders one wind-strength number; gusts visibly ripple the canopy because flutter amplitude and lean both scale with it.

LIGHT: SUN, CLOUDS, GLINTS

  • Sky: a fullscreen shader raycasts each pixel: gradient sky, sun disc + glow, and two octaves of drifting value-noise clouds sampled on a high plane, with silver-lining brightening near the sun.
  • The cloud trick: the same 256² noise data lives twice — as the GPU texture the sky shader draws, and as a JavaScript array. Each frame the CPU samples the cloud density on the sun→tree line and passes a sunVisibility uniform; every material multiplies its direct light by it. So when you see a cloud reach the sun, the ground really darkens, leaf glints die down, and the shadow softens — all from one shared noise field.
  • Leaf shading: per-leaf diffuse from its fluttering normal, a translucency term (leaves between you and the sun glow lime from behind), and a Blinn–Phong glint (pow(N·H, ~90)) with per-leaf gloss — the flutter constantly sweeps normals through the highlight, which is exactly why real trees sparkle.
  • Ground: noise-blotched grass, sun + sky ambient, a soft elliptical blob shadow under the canopy (strength follows sun visibility), and distance haze that fades the hill into the horizon colour.

APPROXIMATIONS & LIMITATIONS

  • No real shadowing: the canopy shadow is a painted ellipse, leaves don't shade each other or the branches, and there's no dappled light on the ground.
  • The wind is kinematic — sines and noise, not fluid dynamics or spring physics, so branches never load up and release the way heavy limbs do in strong gusts.
  • Leaves are flat quads with alpha-tested edges: crisp, but hard-edged against the sky (no soft translucent silhouettes), and they pop rather than bend.
  • Clouds are a flat 2D field on one altitude plane — no volume, no cloud bottoms catching light differently at the horizon.
  • Branch normals aren't re-bent by the sway displacement, so bark lighting is slightly stale mid-gust (invisible in practice at these amplitudes).
  • One tree, one LOD: ~210 branch tubes + ~3,000 leaf instances every frame regardless of distance.

TOOLING

Hand-written single-file HTML/JS/GLSL — four draw calls (sky, ground, branches, leaves), no libraries, no assets; the tree, leaf sprite, noise field and hill are all generated in the page at load.
