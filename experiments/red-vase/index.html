THE DRAGON VASE — HOW IT WORKS

WHAT YOU'RE LOOKING AT

A transparent ruby-glass vase with a gold dragon emblem winding once around its belly, standing on a grained wooden table in a softly lit room. Drag horizontally to spin the vase (it keeps momentum), drag vertically to raise or lower your eye, pinch or scroll to move closer. Leave it alone for a few seconds and it turns slowly on its own. The point of the experiment is the glass: the room refracts through it, thick regions drink the light down to deep ruby, and the rim catches mirror-like reflections at grazing angles.

THE RENDER PASSES (WEBGL2, NO LIBRARIES)

  • Pass 1 — the room: backdrop gradient, the wooden table, a soft contact shadow and a faked red caustic pool are drawn into an offscreen framebuffer. This texture is what the glass will later "look through".
  • Pass 2 — the far side of the vase: the vase's back faces are rendered into a second framebuffer storing their surface normal and texture coordinate per pixel, with a depth texture alongside. This is how the front pass will know how thick the glass is and what the exit surface looks like.
  • Pass 3 — composite: the room is drawn to the screen, then the vase's front faces are shaded with the full glass model (below) and composited on top.

THE GLASS MODEL

  • Refraction, twice: each pixel's view ray is bent entering the glass (refract(), IOR 1.5), marched to the stored back-face depth to find the exit point, bent again at the stored back-face normal (total internal reflection falls back to a reflected ray), and the resulting direction picks the pixel to sample from the Pass-1 room texture. This is screen-space refraction — the standard real-time approximation.
  • Colour: Beer–Lambert absorption, T = exp(−σ·d), with σ small in red and large in green/blue, applied over the per-pixel glass thickness d. Thin rim = pale rose, thick silhouette chords = deep ruby. This is why the edges of the vase look darker red.
  • Reflection: Schlick's Fresnel approximation blends in a procedural environment map (a painted equirectangular canvas of the room: warm lamp glow, cool window strip, dark floor) so grazing angles go mirror-like while face-on glass stays transparent.
  • Highlights: two analytic lights — a warm key and a cool fill — add tight specular lobes on both the glass and the gold.
  • The dragon: lives in a 2048×1024 texture wrapped around the vase (drawn procedurally, see below). On the front surface it shades as embossed metal: the emblem's alpha gradient perturbs the surface normal (screen-space derivatives) so the gold looks raised, lit by the same environment and key lights. Where the far side's emblem shows through the glass, it is shaded flat and tinted by the ruby absorption — you can watch the dragon's back half swim behind the refraction as the vase turns.

PROCEDURAL EVERYTHING

  • Vase: a lathe surface — a hand-tuned amphora profile (foot, belly, neck, flared lip) swept 360° into ~17k triangles, with per-vertex normals from the profile slope.
  • Wood: a 1024² canvas painted per-pixel: long-grain sine bands warped by fractal value noise, colour-mixed between dark and honey oak, plus fine speckle. A grain- dependent gloss term in the table shader gives the sheen streaks.
  • Dragon: painted with 2D canvas calls at load: a Catmull-Rom spine wound once around the texture, a tapered ribbon body built from stamped circles, scale arcs, dorsal spikes, belly scutes, clawed legs, horned head, whiskers, and a flaming pearl. Gold tones with darker outlines so the emboss shading has gradients to bite on.
  • Environment: a small equirectangular canvas (lamp pool, window strip, floor/ceiling gradient) sampled by reflection vectors.

APPROXIMATIONS & LIMITATIONS

  • Screen-space refraction can only sample what Pass 1 rendered: rays that exit toward things outside the frame clamp to the texture edge, and the refracted image is of a flat "photo" of the room, not a true light path.
  • The nearest back face stands in for the true exit surface; around the neck and lip, where a ray really crosses the glass four times, this underestimates the path.
  • The red caustic on the table is a painted decal, not computed photon focusing; it doesn't move when the vase spins (the lights don't either, so it holds up).
  • No reflection of the vase in the tabletop, and the glass casts no true shadow — the contact shadow is baked.
  • The emblem texture seam (where the wrap meets) can flicker for a pixel column when seen through the glass, because the interpolated texture coordinate crosses the wrap.
  • No chromatic dispersion — one IOR for all wavelengths (absorption, not refraction, does the colouring).

TOOLING

Hand-written single-file HTML/JS/GLSL. No 3D library, no image assets — geometry, wood, dragon and environment are all generated in the page at load.
